雨よけ栽培 · Rain shelter

A clear roof so rain never decides

Fixed roof: Stage 1 · motor: later

Rain shelters (雨よけ) are clear roofs over the crop with open sides. Japanese tomato growers use them because a sudden soak makes fruit split and wet leaves spread disease.

The Japanese evidence

  • Rain-shelter (雨よけ) tomato culture is standard Japanese extension practice: it cuts fruit cracking and leaf disease by keeping rain off. NARO · Gifu Pref. agriculture. Reported gains in marketable fruit vary by trial and season; treat any single figure as indicative.
  • Splitting comes from roots taking up a rush of water after dry spells, so keeping rain off and drip-watering evenly removes the main trigger.

How the AI system handles it

  1. Now (Stage 1)

    A fixed clear polycarbonate roof on a hardwood frame over the whole bed. Rain stays off, so every drop comes from the drip line the app controls.

  2. Now

    The app reads the Bureau of Meteorology forecast and skips or adds watering accordingly; heatwave alerts remind you to clip on shade cloth.

  3. Later (optional)

    A motorised clear roll-out roof would let the app open the roof for sun and close it before rain, using the forecast plus a rain and wind sensor. Not built; priced as a later stage.

What you need

Clear corrugated polycarbonate (~6 m²), 70×45 hardwood rafters, 2 front posts anchored to the concrete, gutter, possum net. About $360 at Marketplace prices.

Honest limits

A fixed roof is simpler and much cheaper than a moving one, but it also shades a little. Clear sheet loses only a few percent of light.
