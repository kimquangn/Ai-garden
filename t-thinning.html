摘果 · Fewer fruit per plant

Fewer fruit, more flavour in each

Manual now · camera prompt: later

Hardware: Stage 1 being built · App: in development, nothing is live yet · Controller: Kim's custom local app talking to the ESP32

Japanese melon growers keep one fruit per vine; strawberry and tomato growers remove the smallest fruit and flowers. Fewer fruit means each gets more of the plant's sugar.

The Japanese evidence

  • Crown Melon (Shizuoka) is grown one vine, one fruit; its top grade needs at least 14 °Brix. Crown Melon
  • Strawberry growers remove late small flowers (摘花) and tomato growers trim trusses (摘果) so each remaining fruit gets more sugar; prefectural strawberry guides recommend limiting fruit per truss. NARO
  • The source–sink effect of thinning on fruit size and sugar is well established in horticulture. The home-garden gain is not measured; that is what this bed tests.

How the AI system will handle it

  1. By hand

    You thin by hand: pinch strawberry runners and the smallest end flowers; on tomatoes, trim very long trusses to 8–10 fruit.

  2. Later (camera)

    A cheap plant camera will take a daily photo. The app will watch growth and give a rough heads-up when it's about the right time to thin, e.g. "trusses are setting; check this week". It will be a timing prompt, not per-fruit instructions.

  3. Learn

    Fruit-set dates and Brix at harvest will be logged, so plants thinned differently can be compared season to season.

What you need

Hands and snips now. Camera later: ESP32-S3 camera board, about $23–60.

Honest limits

Camera prompts are approximate and will improve with more photos. You always decide what to remove.
